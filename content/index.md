A review of financial stress has a core problem, which anyone might face, regarding terminology. Such issue may arise specially when considering a long time window -- as in our review, as we didn't set any limit -- which makes possible to examine how vocabulary changed over decades. Also, in order to cover different research questions and conceptualizations, a broad scope of populations and countries were considered. In total, 137 articles were included, totalizing more than 540k participants (apart from big data papers) and over 60 countries. Considering all studies, 17 terms related to financial stress were defined, and different models, using at least one of 38 theories -- a complex interface between psychology, sociology, anthropology, and economics --, were tested alongside 19 formal scales. Detailed results with key findings, scales, and others are presented on [Table 1](https://docs.google.com/spreadsheets/d/1XYcGWjhJC_HMb439b3zm8TlMTVB1UIGwiGTbEiYgEjE/edit?usp=sharing).

The most prevalent design was cross-sectional (n=84, 61.3%). Almost 45% of studies included participants from USA (n=61), followed by Australia (n=9, 6.6%) and Netherlands (n=5, 3.6%). The main unit of analysis was individual adults (n=54, 39.1%), next to countries (n=20, 14.6%) and households (n=14, 10.1%). 2021 was the most prevalent publication year in our sample. Check out the number of publications by year on [[Figure 1.png|Figure 1]]. The most cited word on titles was "financial" (n=147), followed by "stress" (n=88) and "health" (n=21).  The bigram "financial stress" was mentioned 74 times, above "financial hardship" (n=12) and "financial strain" (n=10).
