Suggests financial markets consist of diverse traders operating in different scales of time, each with distinct investment strategies, risk tolerances, and decision-making processes. This complexity lead to difficult to predict volatility patterns and price dynamics, observed in real markets. Such heterogeneity explains volatility clustering, far-talied return distributions, and the persistence of trends across multiple timescales.

Proposed by Ulrich Müller, 1993
