## Definition
Financial stress in economy doesn't have a common definition, but some authors agree on the broad definition that financial stress is the disruption to the normal function of financial markets [[@monin2019]]. Other definitions financial stress are:
- Materialization of a [[Systemic financial risk]] [[@monin2019]], [[@hollo2012]]
- Product of the interaction between vulnerabilities [[@monin2019]]
- Aggregate of brief and prolonged pressures or shocks applied to each element of the system over time [[@oet2015]]
- Force exerted on economic agents by uncertainty and changing expectations of loss in financial markets and institutions [[@illing2006]]
	- They extend the definition by suggesting that the stress results in an altered economic behaviors (which can be linked to [[Financial stress in psychology]]) that have adverse effects on the real economy
- Interruptions in financial markets' normal functioning [[@hakkio2009]]
- Episodes when the financial system is under strain and its ability to intermediate is impaired [[@balakrishnan2009]]
- As a synonym of systemic stress, an instability in the financial system as a whole [[@hollo2012]]
## Theoretical frameworks
Financial stress in economy isn't currently based on a theory, instead it's focused on detecting at least one of these [[@monin2019]], [[@cardarelli2011]], [[@hakkio2009]]:
- Increased asymmetry of information 
- Increased uncertainty about fundamental values of assets
- Increased uncertainty about the behavior of other investors
- Decreased willingness to hold illiquid assets (flight to liquidity)
- Decreased willingness to hold risky assets (flight to quality)
## How it's measured
Financial stress in economy is commonly measured through an index, called Financial Stress Index, or FSI for short. The FSI is a univariate timeseries that aggregates information from a set of indicators that measure the severity and nature of stress as it occurs. The extent to which indicators deviate from some long-term trend should produce a meaningful historical comparison of stress severity. Many authors suggested different FSIs according to their goal and scope, since the indicators can change accordingly to countries and regions, as well as the methods of aggregation.
#### Indicators of financial stress in economy
All the used indicators in the literature until now are measures of:
- Financial volatility [[@monin2019]], [[@huotari2015]]
- Credit spreads [[@monin2019]], [[@oet2015]], [[@huotari2015]]
- Funding spreads [[@monin2019]], [[@oet2015]]
- Interest rates [[@monin2019]], [[@huotari2015]]
- Equity valuations [[@monin2019]], [[@oet2015]], [[@huotari2015]]
- Safe assets [[@monin2019]]
- Securitization [[@oet2015]]
- Real Estate [[@oet2015]]
- Foreign exchange [[@oet2015]], [[@huotari2015]]
#### Methods of aggregation
The methods of aggregation of indicators vary by weighting scheme, which include:
- Dynamic principal component analysis weights [[@monin2019]]
- Static principal component analysis weights  [[@oet2015]], [[@huotari2015]]
- Dynamic credit weights  [[@oet2015]]
- Portfolio theoretic weights [[@oet2015]], [[@huotari2015]]
- Equal market weights [[@oet2015]]
- Variance-equal weights [[@huotari2015]]
