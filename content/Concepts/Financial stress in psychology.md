## Definition
Financial stress is the inability to meet one's financial obligations [[@northern2010]] and [[@heckman2014]]. It's a different concept from [[Financial stress in economy]].
## Theoretical frameworks
#### Roy Adaptation Theory
The Roy Adaptation Theory was used by [[@heckman2014]] to propose a relationship between financial stressors, financial optimism, financial self-efficacy and demographic variables.
#### Social Causation Theory
When measured by income and financial hardships, financial stress can be related to a context of economic uncertainty, unhealthy lifestyle, bad living environment and other social variables. [[@guan2022]]
#### Psychological Stress Theory
The individual's subjetive perception, as well its expectation, may mediate the relationship between objetive financial stressors and the degree of financial stress. [[@guan2022]].
#### Social Selection Theory
Individuals with poor mental health are at risk of bad financial situations [[@guan2022]]:
- Prone to expend on healthcare
- Decreased productivity => Unemployment => Decreased income
## How it's measured
- [[@heckman2014]] used a simple Likert scale question type: "I feel stressed about my personal finances in general"
## Determinants of financial stress
#### Gender
- In [[@brougham2009]], [[@heckman2014]]: women were more likely to experience financial stress
#### Color
- In [[@heckman2014]]: Black students were less likely to report financial stress
#### Anticipated debt
- In  [[@morra2008]], [[@heckman2014]]: anticipated debt was related to increased financial stress
- Previously tested as:
	- “How much student loan debt do you expect to accumulate by the time you graduate?” [[@heckman2014]]
#### Current debt
- In [[@heckman2014]]: current debt was related to increased financial stress
- Previously tested as:
	- “Do you currently have debt from any source, including student loans, credit cards, car loans, personal loans from financial institutions or from family/friends, or any other type of credit or loans?” [[@heckman2014]]
	- “I pay my bills on time every month.” [[@heckman2014]]
#### Financial impulsivity
- I named this variable, but we can call it something different if needed
- In [[@heckman2014]]: financial impulsivity was related to increased financial stress
- Previously tested as:
	- “I regularly spend more than I have by using credit or borrowing.” [[@heckman2014]]
#### Financial relative deprivation
- In [[@heckman2014]]: negative financial relative deprivation was related to increased financial stress
- In [[@guan2022]]: income inequality at the neighborhood was strongly associated with higher risk of depression
- Previously tested as:
	- “I have enough money to participate in most of the same activities as my peers do.” [[@heckman2014]]
#### Financial anxiety
- [[@archuela2013]]: increased financial anxiety lead to increased financial stress
#### Depression
- Although this relation may be bidirectional, it's worth citing depression possibly as either a determinant of financial stress or as a mediator between the aforementioned variables:
	- [[@guan2022]]'s review pointed that there's a positive association between depression and mortgage debt, unsecured debt, debt stress, debt load change, financial hardship, asking for financial help, perceived financial strain at childhood and debt/asset ratio; and a negative association with depression and income/wealth
